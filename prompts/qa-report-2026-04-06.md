## Medium issues

### M1. Registration deadline has already passed with no UX indicator
- **Where:** Election Info tab → Voter Registration section
- **What:** `data/election.json` lists `registrationDeadline: "2026-04-02"`. Today is April 6, 2026. The site renders this as: *"Registration deadline: Thursday, April 2, 2026"* with a link to **Register to Vote (VoteTexas.gov)**. There's no indication the deadline has already passed.
- **Why it matters:** A voter arriving today who hasn't registered may click the link thinking they still can, then feel misled. For a nonpartisan voter guide, you want to be honest about actionability.
- **Suggested fix:** In `renderElectionInfo()` in `app.js`, compare the deadline to today. If in the past, render it as **"Registration deadline: April 2, 2026 (already passed)"** and add a note: *"Registration for this election has closed. You can still register for future elections."* Keep the link for future-election value.

### M2. Connie Rossi's Recent Activity summary has an inline URL artifact
- **Where:** Place 4 → Connie Rossi → Recent Activity
- **What:** The summary text contains this parenthetical: *"On her campaign website (connieforjv.org), she outlines priorities including..."* This is the same inline-URL pattern stripped from Brittain, Beasley, McCauley, and Gill during earlier manual cleanup, but it was left in Rossi's summary.
- **Why it matters:** Inconsistency between candidates looks unpolished. The front end already renders her campaign website as a cited source with a proper title and hostname, so the inline text is redundant.
- **Suggested fix:** Edit `data/social/connie-rossi.json`, change *"On her campaign website (connieforjv.org), she outlines priorities..."* to *"On her campaign website, she outlines priorities..."*. Single deletion.

### M3. Accessibility — tabs have `role="tab"` and `aria-selected` but no `aria-controls`
- **Where:** Race nav tabs, candidate tabs, detail tabs (all three tablists)
- **What:** `app.js` creates buttons with `role="tab"` and toggles `aria-selected`, but none of the tabs have `aria-controls` pointing to their corresponding panel. The panels have `role="tabpanel"` but no `id` that tabs reference, and no `aria-labelledby` back to the tabs.
- **Why it matters:** Screen readers rely on `aria-controls`/`aria-labelledby` to announce which panel a tab controls and to associate them programmatically. Without it, a screen reader user can navigate between tabs but doesn't automatically hear what panel they just activated. WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices require this for a conformant tab pattern.
- **Suggested fix:** In `app.js`, give each tab a deterministic `id` (e.g. `tab-race-place-1`, `tab-candidate-michael-brittain`, `tab-detail-bio`) and an `aria-controls` attribute pointing to the panel's id. Add matching `aria-labelledby` on the panels.

### M4. Accessibility — no keyboard arrow-key navigation between tabs
- **Where:** All three tablists
- **What:** Tabs can be reached via Tab key (they're buttons) and activated via Enter/Space, but the WAI-ARIA tab pattern expects **Left/Right arrow keys** to move focus between tabs in a tablist. There's no keyboard listener for arrow keys in `app.js`.
- **Why it matters:** Power users and many screen reader users expect arrow-key navigation within a tablist. Missing it is a minor conformance gap but noticeable in real assistive tech.
- **Suggested fix:** Add a `keydown` handler on each tablist container that intercepts `ArrowLeft`/`ArrowRight` and shifts focus and selection to the adjacent tab. ~20 lines of JS.

## Minor issues and polish suggestions

### P1. Only one polling location listed
- **Where:** Election Info tab → Early Voting and Election Day sections
- **What:** Both sections list only **Jersey Village City Hall** (16327 Lakeview Dr). Texas voters in Harris County can typically vote at *any* countywide early voting location, not just the one in their city.
- **Why it matters:** Voters may think they have to come to City Hall when they actually have dozens of options. Underselling convenience can suppress turnout.
- **Suggested fix:** Add a note: *"Harris County voters can cast early ballots at any countywide vote center during early voting. See [harrisvotes.com](https://www.harrisvotes.com/) for the full list."* For Election Day, same thing applies under Harris County's vote-anywhere rules.

### P2. Connie Rossi's Public Safety position has slightly promotional phrasing
- **Where:** Connie Rossi → Positions → "Public Safety"
- **What:** *"She has stated that exceptional first responders are what make Jersey Village a safe and welcoming community..."* The phrasing "exceptional first responders" and "safe and welcoming community" reads as paraphrased campaign copy rather than neutral third-party description.
- **Why it matters:** For a nonpartisan voter guide, language should describe what a candidate advocates without adopting their marketing frame. None of the other four candidates have this issue — theirs consistently use neutral distancing ("believes", "argues", "supports").
- **Suggested fix:** Rewrite to: *"She has stated that strong first-responder staffing is essential to keeping Jersey Village among the safest communities in the county."* Same factual content, less adjective-heavy.

### P3. Brittain's Recent Activity mentions "a new municipal and community center"
- **Where:** Michael Brittain → Recent Activity
- **What:** The summary reads: *"...working within the city's existing roadmap for economic development, future land use, and a new municipal and community center."* Jersey Village voters recently rejected a $21.6M City Hall/community campus bond in November 2025. Reference to "a new municipal and community center" could confuse readers about whether this project is still on the table.
- **Why it matters:** Mild risk of voter confusion between the failed 2025 bond and current planning. Not inaccurate — Brittain is quoting the city's existing roadmap — just not fully contextual.
- **Suggested fix:** Either add a brief clarifier ("referring to the city's long-range facilities roadmap") or drop that clause entirely and let his Position #4 carry the economic-development framing.

### P4. Attribution line in footer may not match reality
- **Where:** Footer
- **What:** *"Data is updated daily. Candidate social media summaries are generated using AI and may not capture all activity."*
- **Why it matters:** The automated daily cron is currently **disabled** — content is refreshed manually via the Claude.ai prompt workflow, not daily. "Data is updated daily" is aspirational but not strictly accurate.
- **Suggested fix:** Soften to: *"Data is refreshed regularly. Candidate activity summaries are generated using AI and may not capture all recent activity."* Or tie it to the actual last-updated timestamp.

### P5. Header disclaimer is duplicated verbatim in footer
- **Where:** Header and footer
- **What:** The full 3-sentence disclaimer appears in the header *banner* just below the countdown AND again in the footer. Both render the same text from `election.json`.
- **Why it matters:** Not a bug — redundancy is defensible for a legal/trust statement — but the header placement takes up noticeable vertical space on mobile. Consider shortening the header version to just "Nonpartisan voter guide — not affiliated with any candidate" and keeping the full text only in the footer.
- **Suggested fix:** Optional. If you want to tighten: add a `disclaimerShort` field to `election.json` for the header, keep the full `disclaimer` for the footer.

### P6. No `<noscript>` fallback
- **Where:** `index.html`
- **What:** The entire site requires JavaScript to render anything (all content is built from JSON by `app.js`). There's no `<noscript>` message for users who have JS disabled or blocked.
- **Why it matters:** Small percentage of users, but a voter info site should have SOMETHING useful even with JS disabled.
- **Suggested fix:** Add a `<noscript>` block inside `<main>` with a short message and the key election date/location info hardcoded, e.g. *"This site requires JavaScript. Key information: Election Day is May 2, 2026. Early voting April 20-28. See harrisvotes.com for polling locations."*

### P7. "Updates daily" text on Recent Activity status line
- **Where:** Every candidate's Recent Activity tab
- **What:** The always-visible status line reads: *"Last checked: April 6, 2026 · Updates daily"* — same "daily" claim as the footer.
- **Why it matters:** Same reason as P4 — the cron is disabled and updates are actually manual.
- **Suggested fix:** Change the hardcoded string in `app.js` (in `renderSocial`) from `Updates daily` to `Updates regularly` or `Refreshed as news breaks`.

### P8. No favicon
- **Where:** Browser tab
- **What:** `index.html` has no `<link rel="icon">`. Browsers will show a default globe/blank icon.
- **Why it matters:** Tiny thing but noticeable when the site is pinned or bookmarked. For a civic site, a TX-flag icon would match the header "TX" badge.
- **Suggested fix:** Add `<link rel="icon" href="data:image/svg+xml,...">` with a simple SVG TX badge. Or drop a `favicon.ico` in the repo root.

### P9. Page `<title>` is generic
- **Where:** Browser tab / search results
- **What:** Title is *"Jersey Village 2026 Election Guide"* which is fine but doesn't include the date. For time-sensitive content, including "May 2, 2026" in the title helps search results and bookmarks.
- **Suggested fix:** *"Jersey Village 2026 Election Guide — May 2 Voter Information"*

## Specific praise

### What's working really well

1. **Substantive, cited content for every candidate.** Every bio is 7+ sentences with real biographical detail. Every candidate has 4-5 position statements. Every Recent Activity summary has 3-7 source links with descriptive titles and hostnames. This is dramatically better than most local voter guides.

2. **Honest acknowledgment of unverified information.** Three of the five bios explicitly say *"additional verified details ... were not independently confirmed through available public sources."* This is exactly the right posture for a neutral voter guide — confident where the facts are solid, honest where they aren't. It builds trust.

3. **Balanced Proposition A presentation.** Four for-arguments, four against-arguments, both substantive and specific. Clearly states this is a renewal, not a new tax. No subtle framing toward yes or no. Textbook nonpartisan.

4. **Strong nonpartisan disclaimer prominently displayed.** *"This website is an independent, nonpartisan voter information resource. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or funded by the City of Jersey Village, any candidate, or any political party."* Shown in both header and footer. First thing a visitor sees. Removes any ambiguity.

5. **Persistent candidate header across detail tabs.** When you switch between Bio / Positions / Contact / Recent Activity, only the content panel below changes — the candidate's avatar, name, and race label stay in place. Eliminates the visual jitter that was present earlier in development. Professional polish.

6. **Fixed-width card layout.** The card doesn't resize between tabs (`width: 100% + max-width: 960px` on `.container`, `min-height: 280px` on `.detail-panel`, `flex: 1` on detail tabs). Shows the team cared about small details.

7. **Source citation quality.** Every Recent Activity source has a platform badge (Web/Facebook/X), a descriptive page title, and the hostname. Better than most professional news sites that just show bare URLs.

8. **Typography for long-form bios.** Max-width 68ch, line-height 1.8, 0.97rem font — comfortable for reading 7+ sentence biographies on both desktop and mobile.

9. **Countdown timer with auto-refresh.** The "26 days until Election Day" banner updates every minute via `setInterval`. Small touch, but signals the site is active and current.

10. **`prefers-reduced-motion` support.** The CSS disables animations for users who've set that preference. Good accessibility citizenship.

11. **Reusable Claude.ai prompt workflow.** Five named prompts in `prompts/` (including this QA one) that any future maintainer can use to refresh or validate content without touching code. Makes the site sustainable long after the original author moves on.

12. **Honest tech choices.** Vanilla HTML/CSS/JS, no build step, no framework, no backend. A voter in 2030 can still clone this repo and understand it. A lot of "modern" civic sites rot when their framework goes out of fashion; this one won't.
